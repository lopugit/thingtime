2 · Anatomy

The same four ingredients as a timeline row, arranged for time-travel. The line answers "when"; the frame answers "what did it look like".

01 · line

Nodes on one line, true to time

A hollow node per version, coloured by verb; labels alternate above and below so nothing collides; small dots are moments (a sync, an attachment) that explain the line without making a version. The day axis sits quietly beneath, and the line brightens toward now.

02 · card

The Thing, rendered as of then

In Cards and Frames, a page shows its heading, folder, block structure and attachments at that version; a Component renders its actual button; a record lists its fields; a post shows its text and audience. Cards carry an edit callout (what changed) and hang alternately above and below the spine; Frames put the chips and "2 days later" on the connectors. In Line, the selected version renders in the panel below.

03 · lanes

Variations branch below

A private variation runs on a dashed purple lane from its starting version. "Needs review" marks the shared version that conflicts with it; Review and combine resolves it without losing either.

3 · One browser: scope × look

There is one History browser. Scope chooses what is on the timeline; Look chooses how it is drawn; every combination shares the filters, the day scrubber and the detail panel. Variations (private branches) are lanes inside every look — not a separate view — and named versions get a pin.

Scope

What is on the timeline

Everything across your Things, This Thing, or a Page + related (the Components and records it renders). Kind, who, what and sync-state chips narrow it further.

Rendering as-of is the whole trick: a timeline-event carries the property diffs, so the client folds them forward from the Thing's first version to produce each frame — cheap for pages, Components and records, and honest when a dependency's version is unknown (the frame says so instead of guessing). Real-time spacing uses true positions along the line and spreads only the nodes that would collide, so the axis stays proportional everywhere else. Entry points are unchanged: the ⋯ menu history verb, the editor's Changes control and card chips open the browser scoped to the Thing, and the Evolution switch is one click away.
Cards alternate above and below so neighbours never overlap; the rainbow spine fills to the selected version, later cards wait faded until you reach them, and the minimap shows where you are in the whole history. Below 700px the strip keeps scrolling horizontally (cards 240px, labels 124px); Frames snap per card. Motion respects prefers-reduced-motion. Fictional data throughout.
